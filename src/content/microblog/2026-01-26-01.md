  Late at night, staring absently at the two faces on my desk, I suddenly noticed a coincidence that may not be mere coincidence.
  <br/>
  It turns out that whenever I choose a figure, I have only ever looked at the eyes.
  <br/>
  Rei Ayanami, an artificial being supposedly without emotion, turns back to meet a glass of water with a smile as gentle as water itself.

  Guanyin, a bodhisattva beyond human feeling, lowers her gaze in compassion, pure and aloof as the moon.
  <br/>
  The first scroll of the Xuanhe Catalogue of Paintings opens with an account of Gu Kaizhi: whenever he completed a portrait, he might leave the pupils undotted for years. Asked why, he replied, “The beauty or plainness of the limbs has no bearing on the subtle essence. To transmit the spirit in a likeness—it is all there, in the eyes.”

  Song sculptors gave Guanyin the breath of a living person entirely through those eyes: eyes like the moon seen in water.

<br/>

原来我买造像 从来都只看眼睛的——

<br/>
