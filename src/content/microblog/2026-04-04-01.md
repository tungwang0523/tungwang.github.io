  With little else to do over the holiday, I wrote a small tale
  in the manner of a classical Japanese monogatari.

  Long, long ago, in April of some distant year,
  a traveller from the Tang lands journeyed along the San’yōdō.
  When his travels brought him to Maiko-no-hama,
  from his ox carriage he saw a maiden standing upon the shore.

  She wore a robe of sky blue,
  and in her left hand held aloft a gleaming bronze mirror.
  Facing the sunlight, she smiled at the traveller from the Tang lands.

  Such was the maiden’s beauty
  that even Princess Kaguya of the Moon,
  upon seeing her, would surely have hidden her face behind a cypress fan in shame.
  What hope, then, had an ordinary mortal man?

  The traveller was just leaning his head beyond the ox carriage,
  hoping for one more glimpse of her matchless face,
  when the maiden vanished without a trace.

  For the rest of the journey,
  he dared not draw his head back into the carriage for even a moment,
  lest he miss the maiden’s reappearance.
  Yet all the way to Himeji, he never saw her again.
  He learned only that her name was Akashinotohime (Princess of the Akashi Strait).

  After sailing home to the Tang lands,
  the traveller spent his days staring out to sea.
  How many times the sun rose and set beyond the horizon;
  how many times the tides advanced and withdrew along the shore.
  Still he could not forget the face of Akashinotohime,
  and even the sea before him seemed drained of colour.

  The traveller sent many envoys across the water to Akitsushima
  to seek news of Akashinotohime.
  But he had remembered the hand that held her bronze mirror as the right, not the left,
  and one envoy after another departed without reply.

  So he waited until the seventh month.
  Then the traveller resolved to sail to Akitsushima himself.
  He went nowhere else, but ordered his boat to put in directly at Maiko-no-hama.

  It was a day heavy with cloud.
  Upon the shore, the traveller saw Akashinotohime once more.
  She wore a robe of grey-blue.
  The bronze mirror remained in her left hand, but no longer shone.
  There was no smile upon her face.
  She looked no different from any ordinary woman he had known in the Tang lands.

  The traveller returned home.
  Yet he could never forget the Akashinotohime he had seen at Maiko-no-hama:
  the maiden who held aloft a glittering bronze mirror,
  whose beauty would have made even Princess Kaguya hide her face in shame—
  Akashinotohime.

  He searched every shore beneath heaven,
  the coasts of the Tang lands and the coasts of Akitsushima alike,
  but never again did he behold such a face.

假期无聊仿照日本古典物语写了一则小故事

<br/>

很久很久之前的某年的四月份

有一个从唐国来的人在山阳道上游历

当他游历到舞子之浜这个地方的时候

在牛车上看到海岸上站着一个女孩

<br/>

她穿着一袭天蓝色的衣裳

左手举着闪闪的铜镜

迎着日光 朝着唐国人微笑

<br/>

这个女孩的容颜

相信连月国的辉夜姬看到之后

也要自惭形秽地用桧扇遮住自己的脸

何况是凡世的普通男人呢

<br/>

唐国人正要将头伸出牛车

想要多看一眼女子的绝世容貌

可惜这女子早不见踪影

<br/>

在接下来的一路上

唐国人的脑袋一刻也不敢收回牛车里

生怕再错过这位女子的显现

可直到姬路 唐国人也没再见过这女子一面

只打听到这个女子的名字唤做安香诗乃渡比卖

<br/>

唐国人坐船回到唐国后

整日对着大海发呆

天边日出日落多少回

海岸潮涨潮落多少次

唐国人仍然忘不了安香诗乃渡比卖的容貌

就连眼前的海水也全无了颜色

<br/>

唐国人派了好多使者渡船前往秋津洲打听安香诗乃渡比卖的下落

但是因为唐国人将安香诗乃渡比卖举着铜镜的手记成了右手

派出的使者一个一个都没有回音

<br/>

就这样一直等到七月份

唐国人决定自己撑船前去秋津洲

哪里都不去 直接令船只停靠在了舞子之浜

<br/>

这是个阴云密布的日子

唐国人在海边再一次看到了安香诗乃渡比卖

只见穿着灰蓝色衣裳的她

左手仍然拿着铜镜却暗淡无光

脸上也看不到笑容

和自己在唐国见到的寻常女子没有什么两样

<br/>

唐国人回去了

但他仍然忘不了在舞子之浜见到的

手里举着闪闪铜镜的

有着让辉夜姬见了都自惭形秽的容貌的

安香诗乃渡比卖

<br/>

他寻遍天下的海岸

不管是唐国的海岸还是秋津洲的海岸

再也见不到这样的容颜
