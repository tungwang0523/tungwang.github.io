# ExpoCraft — Narrative Draft

**One-line pitch:** Rebuilding a vanished city so it can be walked again — one source and one block at a time.

**Core idea:** A world exposition is an archive built to vanish. Reconstruction is how the memory stays navigable — and every block must be traceable to a source.

**Reading structure:** seven beats, mirroring the Between Strangers treatment (scene → loss → decision → method → the archive as a living thing → state → boundary).

---

## 01 · Scene

In 2010 I was a child who could not travel to Shanghai. What I had was the online Expo — the first world exposition to put itself on the internet — and inside it, a place that felt real enough to tour again and again. There was even a game, *Future City*, that rebuilt the whole Expo site in 3D and let you walk through it.

That was the first time I understood that a real place could continue to exist as a navigable digital world.

The Expo closed after six months. Most of the pavilions were demolished. The place I had toured as a child no longer existed anywhere — except, faintly, in decaying websites and my memory.

Years later, after building hundreds of structures in Minecraft, I decided to put it back.

## 02 · The loss: a city designed to vanish

Expo 2010 Shanghai assembled architecture, national representation and optimistic futures across both banks of the Huangpu River. It was built to last six months.

When it closed, buildings were dismantled, websites decayed, and the complete spatial experience — the sequence of pavilions, distances, adjacencies, and how it felt to move through it — became harder and harder to recover. Photographs preserved individual buildings. Nobody preserved the whole.

## 03 · The decision: rebuild it

The ambition was personal: if the real Expo site had disappeared, the virtual one could take its place — not as a souvenir, but as a place that can be walked through again.

The discipline came later, and it is the real contribution. A reconstruction of a vanished place is a claim about what was there. The only way to make that claim trustworthy is to make every block answer to a source.

## 04 · Before a block, a source

Six kinds of record feed the build:

- **Official record** — Expo guides, yearbooks, pavilion descriptions, press material and planning documents.
- **Spatial record** — historical maps, sector plans, aerial photography, satellite imagery and coordinates.
- **Visual record** — visitor photographs, construction images, broadcast footage and surviving web pages.
- **Living record** — existing buildings, post-Expo reuse, site visits and comparisons with the current city.
- **Community record** — fan archives, personal recollections and material scattered across discontinued platforms.
- **Recovered web** — Internet Archive captures and mirrored pages used when original resources disappear.

## 05 · Reconstruction is a chain of translation

Every building passes through the same chain: *find → verify → locate → measure → model → convert → build → compare → revise.*

The tools are part of the honesty: Google Earth for location and footprint; plans and photographs for façade rhythm and scale; SketchUp or Blender for intermediate geometry; ObjToSchematic for conversion; Minecraft as the final reconstruction environment.

The goal is not to hide translation. Every step — from incomplete evidence to block-scale simplification — creates decisions, and those decisions should remain visible and revisable.

## 06 · The archive has three horizons

**Stage I — Reconstruct the site.** Research the original park, restore its map, rebuild key buildings and public space. This is the spatial foundation.

**Stage II — Deepen the record.** Locate more plans and images, revise proportions, complete sector-level ground and building information. This is the iterative archive.

**Stage III — Make it inhabitable.** Build interpretation, exploration and participatory construction on top of the reconstructed scene.

The site is 5.29 km² across five principal sectors. As of the current state, the A-zone pavilions, the Expo Axis, the Expo Cultural Center and the Theme Pavilion in the B zone are built; much of the rest remains open.

## 07 · What this is, and what it isn't

The output is more than a map download. It is a spatial archive — a coherent, navigable reconstruction that restores relationships isolated photographs cannot show; a building catalogue connecting each structure to its evidence and revision history; a learning environment; and a participatory project that stays open to correction.

The honesty cuts both ways. A new photograph can correct a window. A recovered plan can move a whole façade. Completion is less important than keeping every reconstruction open to evidence — because the point is not to seal a memory, but to keep the city walkable and the archive accountable.
